## Review notes

A classical Noether result requires a specified action and continuous symmetry; a conservation law follows from the corresponding invariance. Liouville’s theorem concerns preservation of the natural phase-space measure under Hamiltonian flow. A continuity equation can describe compressible flow in a chosen reduced or noncanonical state space, but defining such a space and its measure is not, by itself, a modification of Liouville’s theorem. The proposed Iron-56 fixed point, pressure signs, and numerical examples therefore need explicit definitions, derivations, units, and checks against empirical data before they can be treated as model results.
